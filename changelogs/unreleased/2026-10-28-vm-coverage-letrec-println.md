### Fixed — Local `letrec` no longer gives a wrong answer on the bytecode VM (2026-10-28)

A local `letrec` whose name was also a top-level function gave a different answer under `ailang run --bytecode` and `--strict-bytecode` than on the evaluator: the self-call bound to the top-level function. `benchmark/shadow.ail` from the design doc printed `10` on the evaluator and `1000` on both VM engines, and the strict gate did not catch it. Local `letrec` now compiles natively on the VM. The lowering marks every binding of a local letrec with its whole group (`stmt.VarDecl.RecGroup`), and the compiler gives each member a prototype that shares one capture list, so a self or mutual reference rebuilds the member's closure from the frame's own captures instead of looking the name up at top level. A self tail call is an `OpTailCall` on that closure, so a 1,000,000-iteration local loop runs in constant VM stack. A letrec binding that is not a lambda, or a letrec the lowering meets in expression position, now makes the enclosing function evaluator-only instead of compiling a guess. Measured over the 41 banked model solutions from the CP spike, strict-VM NATIVE went from 31 to 41, including the 4 that crashed under `--bytecode`. Regression fixture: [`examples/runnable/letrec_local_shadow.ail`](../../examples/runnable/letrec_local_shadow.ail), pinned on all three engines by `internal/runner/vm_letrec_test.go`.
